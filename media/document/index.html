The harbour log is one book, written in every night by whoever keeps the light. This report says what goes in it, why, and the two rules that make it worth having.

Why there is a log

When a boat is late, the first question is when she was last seen, and by whom. Memory is no use for that after a week. The log answers it in the time it takes to turn back a page.

It has a second use nobody planned. Read a year at a time, it shows which berths silt up, which months bring fog, and how often the lamp is lit late.

What is written down

Three things, every night, in this order.

WhatHowFor example
When the lamp was litTo the minute, by the clock on the stairLit 17:42
Which boats came inBy name, with the berth and the hourMarigold, North 4, 21:40
What the weather didWind, sea, and how far one could seeSW 5, moderate, 3 miles

A boat whose name cannot be read is written down as she looked: her rig, her colour, which way she went.

The two rules

  1. Write it the same night. A line written the next morning is a guess, and it reads exactly like one that is not.
  2. Never rub anything out. A mistake is struck through once, so that it can still be read, and the right line is written under it.

In short

One book, three things a night, two rules. Everything else about the log is habit, and habits may change with the keeper.
