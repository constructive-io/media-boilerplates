# Recordings

What a clip is cut from. Nothing in this folder but this page is committed:
a recording is long, and it is the person's, not the library's.

## How to record

- **One person, one mood.** The way the voice should sound in everything it
  will say. A clip that laughs gives a voice that is always about to.
- **A quiet room with soft things in it.** A bedroom is better than a kitchen.
  Turn off the fan, the fridge and the music. A clone copies a hiss or an echo
  as readily as the person.
- **A phone is enough.** A hand's width from the mouth and a little to one
  side, so breath does not hit it. Not on speaker, not through a headset.
- **Forty seconds to a minute.** A clip is 15 to 28 seconds of it, and it is
  good to have somewhere to choose from.
- **Stop after sentences.** Take a full breath between them. The clip has to
  end in the quiet after one, and begin after another.
- **Any format.** `.wav`, `.m4a`, `.mp3`, a voice memo, the sound of a video.

## Something to read

Reading is fine, as long as it sounds like talking. This has most of the
sounds of English in it, and every sentence is a place a clip could end:

> The ferry leaves at seven, whether or not anybody is on it.
>
> I usually take the bench by the rail, because the view is better and
> nobody minds if I say nothing.
>
> Halfway across, the engine changes its mind about something, and the whole
> boat shivers for a moment.
>
> Then the far shore comes up out of the weather: a church, a yellow crane,
> and a row of houses the colour of old paper.
>
> By the time we tie up I have finished my coffee and thought of three
> things I should have said yesterday.
>
> It is a short crossing, and I have never once wished it were shorter.

Your own words are better still: a minute about what you did this morning,
said the way you would tell a friend.

## Then

```sh
media reference originals/narrator.m4a    # how long, how loud, what is wrong with it, where it could be cut

```
